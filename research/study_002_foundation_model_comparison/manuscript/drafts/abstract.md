# Abstract

Foundation models are increasingly deployed inside agentic workflows that combine planning, execution, and review stages. Yet provider comparisons often evaluate models as standalone systems, leaving limited evidence about how provider choice interacts with workflow architecture under controlled operational conditions. This study presents a reproducible exploratory benchmark of agentic AI workflow configurations.

A balanced 3 × 3 factorial design compared OpenAI GPT-5.5, Google Gemini 2.5 Pro, and Anthropic Claude Sonnet 4.6 across Basic Agent, Planner–Executor, and Planner–Executor–Reviewer workflows. Thirty enterprise-oriented tasks spanning Knowledge, Reasoning, and Coding categories were executed using frozen prompts, fixed workflow logic, and a common task bank, producing 270 analysis-ready runs. Outcomes included a workflow-generated operational quality proxy, model-reported confidence, cost, duration, token consumption, and call count.

The results, summarized in publication tables and figures, indicate significant provider, workflow, and provider × workflow effects on the operational quality proxy. Workflow architecture showed a medium effect on quality (partial η² = 0.099), while provider selection showed a smaller effect (partial η² = 0.024). For confidence, provider selection showed a medium effect (partial η² = 0.086), while workflow showed a smaller effect (partial η² = 0.027). Operationally, more complex workflows increased cost, duration, and token consumption, while Planner–Executor achieved the highest mean quality proxy.

A measurement-validity audit showed that quality and confidence were identical in non-reviewer workflows and mostly distinct in the reviewer workflow. Therefore, `quality_score` is interpreted as an operational proxy rather than an independent human judgment. The findings show that provider and workflow should be evaluated jointly when designing foundation-model-based agentic systems, and that quality-oriented decisions should be considered alongside cost, latency, and token-use constraints.
