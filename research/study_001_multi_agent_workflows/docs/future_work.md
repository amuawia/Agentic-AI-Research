# Future Work

## Overview

This study provides an initial evaluation of agentic workflow architectures using a controlled experimental design. While the results demonstrate measurable benefits of planning and review mechanisms, several opportunities exist for extending and strengthening the research.

---

## Evaluation Across Multiple Models

The current experiment was conducted using a single underlying language model.

Future research should evaluate the same workflow architectures using additional models such as:

* OpenAI GPT-4o
* Anthropic Claude
* Future Gemini versions
* Open-source models

Comparing workflow behavior across different models would improve the generalizability of the findings and help determine whether the observed improvements are architecture-dependent or model-dependent.

---

## Larger Task Datasets

The experiment used a dataset of 30 enterprise-oriented tasks.

Future studies should expand the dataset to include:

* Hundreds of tasks
* Additional business domains
* Industry-specific scenarios
* Real-world enterprise workflows

Larger datasets would improve statistical confidence and provide a broader evaluation of workflow performance.

---

## Human Evaluation

The current study primarily relied on confidence scores and reviewer-generated assessments.

Future research could incorporate:

* Human experts
* Domain specialists
* Independent evaluators

Human evaluation would provide additional evidence regarding response quality, usefulness, and practical applicability.

---

## Cost and Performance Analysis

This study focused on workflow effectiveness rather than operational cost.

Future work should investigate:

* Token consumption
* API cost
* Execution time
* Workflow latency

Understanding the trade-off between performance improvements and operational costs would be valuable for enterprise adoption.

---

## Advanced Multi-Agent Architectures

Only three workflow architectures were evaluated.

Future studies could investigate more sophisticated designs, including:

* Multi-reviewer systems
* Collaborative agent teams
* Reflection-based agents
* Self-correcting workflows
* Hierarchical planning architectures

These approaches may provide additional improvements for complex reasoning tasks.

---

## Real Enterprise Deployments

The experiment was conducted in a controlled environment using benchmark-style tasks.

Future research should evaluate the workflows within real organizational environments where tasks involve:

* Business processes
* Knowledge management
* Customer support
* Software development teams
* Decision-support systems

Such studies would provide stronger evidence regarding practical enterprise impact.

---

## Long-Term Research Direction

A promising direction for future work is the development of adaptive agentic systems that dynamically select workflow architectures based on task complexity.

Rather than using a fixed workflow for all tasks, future systems could automatically determine whether a task requires a single agent, planning stage, review stage, or a more advanced multi-agent configuration.

This approach could improve both efficiency and performance while reducing operational costs.

---

## Summary

The findings of this study suggest that workflow architecture plays an important role in AI system performance. Future research should expand the scope of evaluation through larger datasets, multiple language models, human assessment, and real-world enterprise deployments to better understand the capabilities and limitations of agentic AI systems.
