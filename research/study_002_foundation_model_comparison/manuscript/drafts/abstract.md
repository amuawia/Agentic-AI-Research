# Abstract

Foundation models are increasingly deployed within agentic workflows that incorporate planning, execution, reflection, and review mechanisms. While prior research has evaluated both foundation models and workflow architectures independently, comparatively little evidence exists regarding how workflow architectures perform across multiple foundation model providers under controlled experimental conditions. This study investigates the relative influence of workflow architecture and provider selection on task performance, confidence, cost, duration, and token consumption.

A controlled experimental design was implemented using three foundation model providers—OpenAI GPT-5.5, Google Gemini 2.5 Pro, and Anthropic Claude Sonnet 4.6—and three workflow architectures: Basic Agent, Planner–Executor, and Planner–Executor–Reviewer. Thirty enterprise-oriented benchmark tasks spanning knowledge, reasoning, and coding domains were executed using identical workflow logic, frozen prompts, standardized evaluation procedures, and a common task bank. The resulting dataset consisted of 270 experimental runs (3 providers × 3 workflows × 30 tasks). Statistical analysis included dataset validation, assumption testing, two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), and effect size estimation.

The results indicate that workflow architecture exerted a stronger influence on quality outcomes than provider selection alone. For quality score, significant effects were observed for provider, workflow architecture, and the provider–workflow interaction, suggesting that workflow effectiveness depends in part on the underlying foundation model. In contrast, provider selection exhibited a stronger influence on confidence scores than workflow architecture, while the interaction effect was not statistically significant. Operational analysis demonstrated substantial increases in cost, execution duration, and token consumption as workflow complexity increased, particularly for Planner–Executor–Reviewer architectures.

Within the experimental setting examined, the findings suggest that workflow design may represent a more important determinant of answer quality than provider selection alone, whereas provider characteristics may exert greater influence on confidence outcomes. The study contributes a controlled cross-provider evaluation of agentic workflow architectures and highlights the trade-offs between effectiveness and operational efficiency that organizations must consider when deploying foundation-model-based systems.
